OBJECTIVE

Reconstruct the interpretive process by which each earlier source is read,
transformed, and used to produce the sugya's arguments and conclusions.

ANALYSIS METHOD

Perform seven passes for each sugya.

PASS 1 — SOURCE INVENTORY

Identify every source that requires interpretation. Reconcile the authority,
argument, and relationship analyses, but verify each source against the bundle.

PASS 2 — FUNCTION

Determine why the source appears and which textual, argumentative, legal,
theological, ethical, or narrative function it performs.

PASS 3 — TEXTUAL ANALYSIS

Identify the exact quoted portion and the Hebrew or Aramaic wording that matters.
Explain:
- plain meaning in context;
- original-source context when available from the inputs;
- ignored or unused portions;
- textual and grammatical observations;
- ambiguities;
- plausible alternative readings.

Do not invent lexical or grammatical claims. When the supplied text is insufficient
for reliable parsing, preserve the schema fields and state the limitation concisely.

PASS 4 — HEBREW / ARAMAIC ANALYSIS

For every materially significant phrase, populate all fields defined by the schema:
phrase, root when reliable, part of speech, grammatical form, literal translation,
plain meaning, rabbinic reading, reason for the reading, alternatives, and effect
on the argument.

Do not create token-level entries for words that do no interpretive work.

PASS 5 — PARTICIPANT INTERPRETATIONS

For every rabbinic participant who materially interprets the source, reconstruct:
- the participant's textual reading;
- logical reasoning;
- why the source is used;
- hermeneutic method;
- argument role;
- confidence.

Do not assign an interpretation to a participant solely because the participant is
mentioned nearby.

PASS 6 — COMPARISON AND TRANSFORMATION

Explain agreements, disagreements, qualifications, harmonizations, reinterpretations,
and limitations among readings. Populate interpretive_logic and textual_transformation
to show the movement from textual observation to inference, logical step, and
conclusion.

PASS 7 — SUGYA CONTRIBUTION

Explain:
- the source's relationship to the argument and sugya;
- its interactions with other sources;
- what fails if it is removed;
- whether a replacement is possible;
- why it matters to the complete interpretive structure;
- the sugya-level interpretive synthesis required by the schema.

ANALYSIS RULES

1. The bundle is the controlling text. Later-stage files provide mappings and
   hypotheses, not independent proof.

2. Do not merely summarize verses, statements, or stories. Explain the interpretive
   mechanism.

3. Distinguish plain reading from the rabbinic reading and explain the reason for
   the difference.

4. Explain why this source and this wording were useful at this exact point.

5. Keep quotations minimal and analysis specific.

6. Preserve all conditional arrays and objects, even when no Hebrew analysis,
   interpretive commentary, alternative reading, interaction, or replacement exists.

FINAL CHECK

Verify that:
- all materially interpreted sources are included;
- Hebrew and grammatical claims are supported and not fabricated;
- every participant interpretation is textually grounded;
- the chain from wording to inference to argument is explicit;
- every exegesis_analysis schema field is preserved.
